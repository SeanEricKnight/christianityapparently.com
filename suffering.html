The hard question

Why does God allow
suffering?

Christianity has things to say about suffering. None of them make suffering small.

If God is good and powerful, why is the world full of cancer, earthquakes, abuse, dementia, war, children dying, prayers that seem unanswered, and losses that permanently divide life into before and after?

There is no tidy explanation.

The Bible never gives one neat reason for every instance of suffering, and I think that is worth saying plainly.

Job demands answers and gets God’s presence instead (Job 38–42). Herod kills the children of Bethlehem, and Matthew quotes “Rachel weeping for her children” (Matthew 2:16–18). Jesus weeps at a grave (John 11:35) and warns with terrifying seriousness against harming little ones (Matthew 18:6; Mark 10:14–16).

The Bible says the world is broken.

Genesis 3 describes creation fractured by human rebellion, and Romans 8:18–23 pictures the whole creation groaning and waiting for restoration.

Human freedom explains some suffering. Stable natural laws help explain why a world capable of meaningful action is also capable of natural disaster. Suffering can sometimes produce courage or compassion. None of those ideas explains every particular tragedy.

The center of Christianity is not God watching suffering from a safe distance.

It puts God on a cross.

Christians believe God entered human suffering in Jesus: betrayal, humiliation, injustice, torture, abandonment, and death.

That does not answer every “why.” It changes the Christian answer to “where is God when it hurts?”

And Christianity makes a future claim.

Resurrection means death is not merely accepted as part of life. It is called an enemy. Revelation 21:4 promises a future in which God personally wipes away every tear.

Christian hope is not that everything happens for an obvious reason we can decode. It is that God can redeem what He does not call good, raise the dead, judge evil, and finally make things right.

But what about right now?

Right now, lament is allowed. Help is allowed. Anger is allowed. Questions are allowed. The Psalms make room for all of it.

So there is no neat reason for why a child suffers. There is a God Christians believe suffers with us, takes evil seriously, and has promised to end it.

If that is all you have

God, I don’t understand this. Please don’t leave me in it.
