The hard question

Why does God allow
suffering?

Christianity has things to say about suffering. None of them make suffering small.

If God is good and powerful, why is the world full of cancer, earthquakes, abuse, dementia, war, children dying, prayers that seem unanswered, and losses that permanently divide life into before and after?

There is no clever sentence that fixes this.

Christians have offered several kinds of answers: human freedom explains some evil; a world with stable natural laws allows both meaningful action and natural disaster; suffering can sometimes produce courage or compassion; Christian hope says evil and death will not have the final word.

Those ideas matter. None explains every individual tragedy.

Christianity does not say suffering is imaginary.

The Bible is remarkably unwilling to keep pain tidy. Job protests. The Psalms accuse and lament. Ecclesiastes stares directly at death and apparent meaninglessness. Jesus weeps at a grave even though He knows what He is about to do.

The center of Christianity is not God watching suffering from a safe distance.

It puts God on a cross.

Christians believe God entered human suffering in Jesus: betrayal, humiliation, injustice, torture, abandonment, death.

That does not answer every “why.” It changes the Christian answer to “where is God when it hurts?”

And Christianity makes a future claim.

Resurrection means death is not merely accepted as part of life. It is called an enemy. Christian hope is not that everything happens for an obvious reason we can decode. It is that God can redeem what He does not call good, raise the dead, judge evil, and finally make things right.

But what about right now?

Right now, lament is allowed. Help is allowed. Anger is allowed. Questions are allowed. And if you cannot carry your faith very far today, you can bring God what remains.

If that is all you have

God, I don’t understand this. Please don’t leave me in it.
