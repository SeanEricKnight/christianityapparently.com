Questions · Christians & church

Why do Christians sometimes behave nothing like Jesus?

Short answer: because Christians are imperfect people, and some who use the name never followed Him at all. That’s not an excuse. Jesus held His followers to a high standard and said people would judge them by it (John 13:34–35). When Christians act cruelly, deceitfully or abusively, they fail that standard, and the failure should be named plainly.

What the New Testament says about this:

  • Jesus expected it. He warned that some who call Him “Lord” will not be known by Him (Matthew 7:21–23), that weeds grow among wheat until the end (Matthew 13:24–30), and He gave His harshest words to religious leaders who burdened people and did not help them (Matthew 23).
  • The early church wasn’t clean either. Paul confronted Peter for hypocrisy (Galatians 2:11–14). He rebuked the Corinthian church for tolerating abuse and for dividing the poor from the rich at communion (1 Corinthians 5; 11:17–22). The letters to the churches in Revelation 2–3 call out churches for cold love and compromise.
  • The test is spelled out. John says anyone who does not love the person they can see cannot claim to love the God they cannot see (1 John 4:20), and Jesus says you recognize people by their fruit (Matthew 7:16–20).

The strongest objection: “They’re not real Christians” can become a dodge. If anyone who behaves badly gets excluded from the category, the claim that Christianity changes people can’t be tested. And the harm is real: abuse covered up by institutions, cruelty justified in Jesus’ name, contempt for outsiders. For someone who has been hurt by Christians, the failure is not an abstract point. I think the objection is fair, and I’d rather admit it than defend the institution. If you’re being harmed, or someone you know is, please contact the proper authorities and not only a church leader.

What I’m not sure about: how much to expect. I do expect real change over a long time, and where it is absent I think that counts against that person’s or that church’s claim to speak for Jesus. I’m less sure that it counts against Jesus Himself, because the question there is whether His teaching and character are what Christians claim. It may help to read the Gospels first, then judge the people by them rather than the other way round. For related questions, see Why are so many Christians hypocrites?, If Christianity changes people, why are Christians often no better than everyone else? and Why has Christianity been involved in crusades, colonialism, abuse, and violence?
