Questions · Jesus & resurrection

Did Jesus predict events would happen within his generation?

Short answer: Jesus is reported as saying, “This generation shall not pass, till all these things be fulfilled” (Mark 13:30; compare Matthew 24:34 and Luke 21:32). The question is what “all these things” refers to, and which events the Gospel writers connect with the destruction of Jerusalem and the future coming of the Son of Man.

What the text says: Mark 13 begins with a prediction about the Temple. The Romans destroyed Jerusalem’s Temple in AD 70, within the lifetime of some who heard Jesus. But the discourse also includes cosmic imagery, tribulation, and the Son of Man, making the scope of the prediction disputed.

Competing interpretations: One reading connects much of the discourse with the events of AD 70 and treats its imagery as prophetic language. Another distinguishes near-term signs from a still-future consummation. Critical scholars also ask whether early Christians expected the final end sooner than it came. Each explanation has to address the whole passage, not just verse 30.

The strongest objection: If Jesus meant the final cosmic consummation would happen within that generation, its nonoccurrence becomes a serious problem. Interpretations that restrict the prediction to the Temple’s fall must explain why the surrounding language can sound broader.

What remains uncertain: Scholars disagree about the relation between Jesus’s historical teaching, the evangelists’ editing, apocalyptic imagery, and the events of AD 70. Test Mark 13 beside Matthew 24 and Luke 21 rather than using one isolated sentence.

Key texts: Mark 13:1–37; Matthew 24:1–36; Luke 21:5–36; Daniel 7:13–14.

Further research: Ian Paul: The coming of Jesus in Mark 13 (compares readings by R. T. France and N. T. Wright)
