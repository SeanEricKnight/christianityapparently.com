Was there really a census under Quirinius?

Short answer: There was a census under Quirinius, but Josephus dates it to AD 6–7, after Herod’s death, which does not fit Luke’s account of a census before Jesus was born under Herod. This is a real unresolved problem.

What should we test? Luke 2:1–2 places the census while Quirinius was governing Syria. Josephus says Quirinius conducted a census of Judea in AD 6/7 after Archelaus was deposed, and Herod died in 4 BC (some argue 1 BC). Check whether Luke could mean an earlier census, and whether Rome required people to return to ancestral towns.

Competing readings: Defenders propose that Luke means “before Quirinius was governor,” that Quirinius served earlier in some role, or that a census began under Herod and was finished later. Critics argue Luke confused two events and that a Roman census would not require travel to ancestral towns.

The strongest objection: Each proposed solution has grammatical or evidential problems. Some are plausible but rest on evidence nobody has found, such as another registration under Herod. Others require unusual readings of Luke’s Greek.

What remains uncertain: Whether Luke made an error, used a source loosely, or reports something we cannot verify. No new evidence has settled the question, and scholars who accept Luke’s general reliability still disagree about this verse.

Further research: David Rhoads, “Josephus Misdated the Census of Quirinius,” JETS (2011), argues the census began under Herod; Ian Paul: Did Luke get his nativity history wrong? (surveys proposed solutions)
