Questions · Bible

How can a good God command the killing of entire peoples?

Short answer: these are some of the hardest texts in the Bible, and Christians do not all explain them the same way. The commands and battle accounts are real parts of the text and should not be waved away.

Some interpreters point out that ancient war language can be sweeping: Joshua sometimes speaks as if populations were totally destroyed, while Judges still describes many of those peoples living in the land. The Bible also says Israel was not chosen because it was morally superior and warns that Israel itself could face judgment (Joshua 10:40 · Judges 1 · Deuteronomy 9:4–5).

The strongest objection: even allowing for rhetoric and ancient context, texts that present God commanding lethal violence are morally disturbing.

What I’m not sure about: I do not think context makes every problem disappear. Christians read the whole Bible in light of Jesus, who teaches love of enemies and tells His followers to put away the sword (Matthew 5:44–45 · Matthew 26:52). Some questions remain open.
