Questions · Bible

How can a good God command the killing of entire peoples?

Christians disagree here too. These texts—Deuteronomy 7:1–2; Deuteronomy 20:16–18; Joshua 6:21; 1 Samuel 15:3—are really hard, and nobody should wave them away.

Interpreters usually point out that ancient war language was often sweeping: Joshua can describe total destruction (Joshua 10:40; Joshua 11:23), while Judges 1 still describes Canaanite populations living in the land. Israel is also explicitly told it was not chosen because it was righteous (Deuteronomy 9:4–5), and similar judgment is threatened against Israel itself (Leviticus 18:24–28; Deuteronomy 28).

Genesis 15:16 portrays judgment as delayed for generations, and Jonah 4:11 shows God’s concern for Israel’s enemies.

Then there is Jesus, who says “love your enemies” (Matthew 5:44–45) and “put your sword back” (Matthew 26:52). Many Christians read the whole Bible through Him. Some of the questions stay open.
