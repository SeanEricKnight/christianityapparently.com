Questions · Christians & church

Why are so many Christians hypocrites?

Short answer: because becoming Christian does not make someone incapable of hypocrisy. Jesus spends a remarkable amount of time attacking religious hypocrisy Himself.

What the New Testament says about it: Jesus expected it. He warned that some who call Him “Lord” will not be known by Him (Matthew 7:21–23), that weeds grow among the wheat until the end (Matthew 13:24–30), and He gave His harshest words to religious leaders who burdened people and did not help them (Matthew 23). The early church was not clean either: Paul confronted Peter for hypocrisy (Galatians 2:11–14) and rebuked the Corinthian church for tolerating abuse (1 Corinthians 5). And the test is plain: Jesus says people will know His followers by their love (John 13:34–35), and John says anyone who does not love the person they can see cannot claim to love the God they cannot see (1 John 4:20).

The strongest objection: “Christians are sinners too” can become a cheap excuse. If the Holy Spirit changes people, there should eventually be some observable difference. And “they’re not real Christians” can become a dodge: if everyone who behaves badly is excluded from the category, the claim can never be tested. The harm is also real, including abuse covered up by institutions and cruelty justified in Jesus’ name. If you are being harmed, or someone you know is, please contact the proper authorities and not only a church leader.

What I’m not sure about: I expect Christianity to produce fruit over time, and I think serious, persistent hypocrisy is evidence worth taking seriously—not something to wave away with a slogan. I am less sure it counts against Jesus Himself, since the question there is whether His teaching and character are what Christians claim. It may help to read the Gospels first, then judge people by them. For related questions, see If Christianity changes people, why are Christians often no better than everyone else? and Why has Christianity been involved in crusades, colonialism, abuse, and violence?
