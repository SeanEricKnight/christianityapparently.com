Questions · Jesus & resurrection

Who were the 500 witnesses in 1 Corinthians 15?

Short answer: We don’t know. Paul says in 1 Corinthians 15:6 that Jesus appeared to more than 500 people at once, most of whom were still alive when he wrote. No Gospel records the event, and no names survive.

What should we test? The passage is early: scholars generally date 1 Corinthians to the mid-50s AD and think the list in 15:3–7 is a tradition Paul received, possibly within a few years of the crucifixion. Compare it with the Gospels, which do not mention such an appearance.

Competing readings: Defenders argue Paul invited readers to check with living witnesses, which would be risky if false. Others suggest it refers to the Pentecost event in Acts 2 or a Galilean gathering, and some scholars see it as a visionary experience shared in a group. Skeptics note that Paul does not describe the event, and that we cannot cross-examine anyone.

The strongest objection: Paul’s statement is real evidence that early Christians claimed a large group experience. It is not an eyewitness account of it. We cannot tell what the people saw, or how many were present.

What remains uncertain: Whether the number is literal, where it happened, and what the experience was. Because no other source describes it, the question cannot be settled with the evidence we have.
