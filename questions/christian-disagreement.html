Questions · Christians & church

If the Holy Spirit guides Christians, why do sincere Christians disagree so much?

Short answer: Christians believe the Holy Spirit guides the church, not that every Christian becomes incapable of misunderstanding anything.

The New Testament itself shows disagreement: Paul confronts Peter, Paul and Barnabas split over a coworker, and Acts 15 records a major dispute that had to be argued through. Paul also says “we know in part” (Galatians 2:11–14 · Acts 15:36–41 · 1 Corinthians 13:9–12).

The strongest objection: if Christians claim divine guidance, centuries of sincere disagreement can make that guidance look indistinguishable from ordinary human interpretation.

What I’m not sure about: I do not think disagreement proves there is no guidance, but it should make Christians less impressed with their own certainty. The New Testament itself tells believers to test claims rather than assume every spiritual-sounding conclusion is right (1 John 4:1).

Did this miss the point? If this answer is confusing, incomplete, or wrong, tell me.
