I want to follow Jesus

Questions · Doubt & motives

What if Christianity feels true because I want it to be true?

Short answer: then you should take that possibility seriously. Wanting something to be true can distort judgment.

But the reverse bias exists too: not wanting Christianity to be true can affect judgment. Motives cut both ways.

The strongest objection: religious experience is especially vulnerable to confirmation bias because the subject is personal, emotional, and hard to measure.

What I’m not sure about: I do not trust “it feels true” as enough. Feelings belong in the evidence pile, but they should not be the entire pile.

Did this miss the point? If this answer is confusing, incomplete, or wrong, tell me.
