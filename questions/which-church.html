Questions · Christians & church

Which Christian church or denomination should I join?

Short answer: I can’t tell you which tradition is right, and I don’t think one answer fits everyone. But I can tell you what to look for. Start by finding a church that is serious about Jesus, honest about its own failures, and a place where you can ask hard questions without being treated as a problem.

The rough map: Christianity has three large streams: Catholic, Orthodox and Protestant. Protestants include Lutheran, Reformed, Anglican, Baptist, Methodist, Pentecostal and many independent churches. They agree on the center: Jesus died and rose (1 Corinthians 15:3–8), and the early creeds describe who He is. They disagree on authority, the sacraments, church government and some doctrines. For the history of those splits, see Why do Catholic, Orthodox, and Protestant Bibles contain different books? and If the Holy Spirit guides Christians, why do sincere Christians disagree so much?

What I’d check before committing:

  • Is Jesus central? Is the Bible actually read and taught, not only quoted? Do they affirm the basics in the Nicene Creed?
  • How do they treat people who fail? Ask how they handle abuse and misconduct, who holds the leaders accountable, and how the money is managed. These are fair questions, and a healthy church answers them plainly (compare 1 Peter 5:1–3, 1 Timothy 3).
  • Can you ask honest questions? Try asking one in the first month. Watch how it’s received.
  • Can you actually belong? Acts 2:42–47 and Hebrews 10:24–25 describe a church where people know each other. Pick one you can reach regularly and where you can meet people.
  • Red flags: pressure to cut off family, secrecy about money, leaders who can’t be questioned, shaming of doubt, or promises that faith will make you rich or well.

The strongest objection: “If every church says it’s right, picking one is arbitrary.” Not entirely. There are real questions behind the differences: Is final authority Scripture alone, or Scripture interpreted by a church with continuity from the apostles? How is baptism understood, and who is it for? If those questions matter to you, they’re worth studying rather than guessing. Visiting two or three churches from different traditions is a perfectly reasonable way to start.

What I’m not sure about: I don’t think there’s a single correct answer on the denominational questions, and I’m wary of people who say there obviously is. There are thoughtful Christians in all these traditions. If you’re new, a good local church you’ll actually attend beats a theoretically perfect one you won’t.
