Why doesn’t the Bible clearly condemn slavery?

Christians disagree about how to interpret these texts. The Bible doesn’t give the simple, universal ban a modern reader would expect, and that’s uncomfortable.

The law regulates slavery (Exodus 21:2–11; Leviticus 25:44–46), and the New Testament addresses slaves and masters inside an existing slave society (Ephesians 6:5–9; Colossians 3:22–4:1; 1 Peter 2:18).

Other texts pull strongly against slavery: kidnapping someone to enslave them is condemned (Exodus 21:16; 1 Timothy 1:10); runaway slaves aren’t to be returned (Deuteronomy 23:15–16); everyone is made in God’s image (Genesis 1:27); Paul tells Philemon to receive Onesimus as more than a slave—as a brother (Philemon 15–16), and says there is neither slave nor free in Christ (Galatians 3:28).

Both abolitionists and slaveholders quoted Scripture. The honest answer is that biblical themes helped undermine slavery, but the Bible never abolished it in one sentence. That doesn’t make the problem go away.
