Why doesn’t the Bible clearly condemn slavery?

Short answer: the Bible never gives the simple universal ban on slavery a modern reader would expect. That is a real moral difficulty.

Its laws regulate slavery, and New Testament letters address enslaved people and masters inside existing slave societies. Other passages cut strongly against slavery: kidnapping people is condemned, runaway slaves are protected, all people bear God’s image, and Paul tells Philemon to receive Onesimus as a brother rather than merely as a slave (Exodus 21:16 · Deuteronomy 23:15–16 · Philemon 15–16 · Galatians 3:28).

The strongest objection: if slavery is wrong, why regulate it instead of simply prohibiting it? Christians have also used the Bible to defend slavery, so this is not merely theoretical.

What I’m not sure about: biblical themes helped Christians argue against slavery, but the Bible never abolishes it in one sentence. I do not think pretending otherwise helps.
