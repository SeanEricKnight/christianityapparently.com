Faith and doubt

What if
I doubt?

Then don’t fake certainty. Bring the doubt with you and keep looking.

Doubt is not one thing. Sometimes it is an intellectual problem. Sometimes it is disappointment wearing an intellectual hat. Sometimes you prayed and nothing happened. Sometimes Christians hurt you. Sometimes you simply realized, “I don’t know if any of this is true.”

Start by naming the actual doubt.

“I have doubts” is too large to examine. What do you actually doubt?

That God exists? That Jesus rose from the dead? That the Bible can be trusted? That God is good? That He hears you? That He could still want you after what you’ve done?

Those are different questions. They deserve different answers.

You are allowed to tell God.

If God is real, He is not learning about your doubt when you say it out loud.

“I believe; help my unbelief.”

That prayer in Mark 9 is not polished certainty. It is faith and doubt occupying the same sentence.

Don’t confuse a feeling with a verdict.

There will be days when God feels near and days when He does not. Neither feeling, by itself, settles whether He is there.

Ask questions. Read. Think. Pray badly if that is the only way you can pray honestly. Talk to thoughtful Christians who are not frightened by questions.

One useful rule: don’t demand certainty from Christianity that you don’t demand from anything else. Look for good reasons, not mathematical proof of every claim.

And don’t protect Christianity from the hard questions.

If something looks contradictory, cruel, implausible, historically doubtful, or morally disturbing, look at it. An answer that survives examination is worth more than one protected from it.
