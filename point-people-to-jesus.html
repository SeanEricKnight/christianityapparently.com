Make it apparent

Point people
to Jesus.

With your words, yes. But also with the kind of life that makes people wonder where the love came from.

Christianity is not supposed to remain an opinion you carry around privately while nothing else changes. If Jesus is who Christians say He is, eventually that should become visible.

Live something worth asking about.

Jesus told His followers to let their light be seen—not so people would admire them, but so attention would move toward God (Matthew 5:16).

He also said people would recognize His disciples by their love for one another (John 13:35).

That means pointing people to Jesus is not only explaining what you believe. It is becoming the sort of person whose patience, mercy, courage, honesty, generosity, repentance, and love make the explanation believable.

If your faith is real, somebody besides you should eventually be able to see it.

That does not mean performing goodness. It means following Jesus when nobody is giving out points for it. Forgive when revenge would be easier. Tell the truth when lying would help. Admit when you are wrong. Notice the person everyone else ignores. Give without needing your name attached.

Not because those things earn salvation. Because grace, when received, begins to leave fingerprints.

And sometimes, say His name.

A life can make the gospel visible, but eventually somebody may ask what you actually believe. You do not need a theology degree or a rehearsed personality transplant.

You can tell the truth plainly: who Jesus is, what He has done, why you trust Him, and what grace has meant in your own life.

You are not required to win the argument. You are not the Holy Spirit with a clipboard.

The Romans Road.

The “Romans Road” is a simple way Christians have used passages from Paul’s letter to the Romans to explain the core movement of the gospel. It is useful because it gives you a path through the message without requiring a speech.

01 / THE PROBLEM

Romans 3:23

All of us have sinned. Christianity starts by putting everyone on the same ground. Nobody arrives because they were the impressive one.

02 / THE CONSEQUENCE

Romans 6:23

Sin leads to death, but God offers eternal life as a gift in Jesus Christ. The contrast matters: wages are earned; a gift is received.

03 / THE LOVE

Romans 5:8

God did not wait for us to become respectable. Christ died for us while we were still sinners.

04 / THE RESPONSE

Romans 10:9–10

The response is trust: confessing Jesus as Lord and believing that God raised Him from the dead. Not merely knowing facts about Jesus, but entrusting yourself to Him.

05 / THE INVITATION

Romans 10:13

The invitation is open: everyone who calls on the Lord can be saved.

It is a road, not a spell.

Those verses are not magic words, and reciting them in the correct order does not save anyone. Jesus does.

The point of the Romans Road is to help someone see the shape of the gospel: our need, God’s grace, Christ’s death and resurrection, and the invitation to trust Him.

If the conversation needs more time, give it more time. If someone has questions, let them ask. If they are hurting, listen before turning their pain into an opening statement.

Then keep living it.

Pointing somebody to Jesus does not end when they understand the gospel. Walk with them. Pray with them. Help them read Scripture. Help them find a healthy church. Keep showing them what grace looks like when people fail and get back up.

And keep doing that yourself.

Look at Jesus. Ask what’s true. Trust Him. Then make it apparent.
