COUNCILS.

For almost 2,000 years, Christians have settled big questions by meeting and arguing. Here is that story in five short steps, then the full record of every council: what it decided, and what popular accounts get wrong.

The short story

HOW CHRISTIANS
argued it out.

  1. Around AD 49 · Jerusalem

    Even the first Christians disagreed.

    Acts 15 describes leaders meeting in Jerusalem to ask whether non-Jewish believers had to follow Jewish law. It is the closest thing the New Testament has to a church council.

  2. Over the centuries

    A council was a meeting to settle a question.

    When a dispute got serious, church leaders, mostly bishops, gathered to argue it out and issue a decision. Some councils were local. A few were meant to speak for the whole church.

  3. AD 325 · Nicaea

    The council everyone has heard of.

    Emperor Constantine brought bishops together at Nicaea, in what is now Turkey, to deal with a dispute over how Jesus relates to God the Father. The bishops affirmed that the Son shares the Father’s own being. Popular legend says they also chose the books of the Bible. The surviving record does not show that.

  4. AD 381–451

    Then came harder questions.

    Later councils asked what to say about the Holy Spirit, and how Jesus can be both divine and human. At Chalcedon in 451 the bishops declared Christ one person in two natures. Not every church accepted that, and some of those divisions still last.

  5. AD 1215 and after

    Councils were also political, and not always kind.

    Several were shaped by emperors, popes and power struggles. Lateran IV, in 1215, imposed anti-Jewish restrictions. Honest history keeps that in view.
