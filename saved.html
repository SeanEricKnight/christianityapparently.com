Grace

What does it mean
to be saved?

You don’t earn salvation. You receive it by trusting Jesus.

Christians use the word “saved” so often that it can become religious wallpaper. Saved from what? By whom? How?

The problem is bigger than bad habits.

Christianity says something has gone wrong between us and God. The Bible calls it sin: not merely individual mistakes, but our deep tendency to turn from God and put ourselves at the center.

We cannot repair that relationship by accumulating enough good behavior to outweigh the bad.

That is where grace enters.

The Christian claim is that God acted first. Jesus lived the life we have not lived, died for our sins, and rose from the dead.

Salvation is not wages. It is gift.

You don’t earn your way to Jesus. You trust Him.

What does trust mean?

Not merely agreeing that Jesus existed. It means entrusting yourself to Him—turning toward Him, receiving His mercy, and beginning to follow Him.

Christians often use the word repentance here. It means turning: away from sin and toward God. Not becoming perfect before you come. Coming because you are not.

Do I need the right prayer?

No formula saves you.

If you believe

Jesus, I believe. Save me.

If you want to believe

Jesus, I want to believe. Show me.

The words don’t save you. Jesus does.

What comes after?

Following Jesus. Prayer. Scripture. Baptism. A church. Learning to love God and your neighbor. Failing, confessing, getting up, and continuing.

Grace is where you begin. It is also how you continue.
