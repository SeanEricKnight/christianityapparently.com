Life issues

I keep doing the thing I said I would stop.

Secrecy likes addiction. Recovery usually requires other people.

Christianity has a word for slavery to destructive desire, but naming it spiritual does not mean you should handle it alone.

Tell the truth to someone.

Pick a trustworthy person who can help you move from private promises to concrete accountability.

Use serious help when the problem is serious.

Depending on the addiction, professional treatment, medical care, a recovery program, or a support group may be important. Prayer is not a reason to avoid competent help.

Failure is not permission to quit.

Repentance is not making a dramatic promise and never struggling again. Sometimes it looks like telling the truth again, getting help again, removing access again, and getting back up again.

If stopping a substance could be medically risky, do not try to white-knuckle withdrawal alone. Get qualified medical help.

You do not have to find treatment alone.

In the U.S., the SAMHSA National Helpline can connect you with treatment and recovery resources at 1-800-662-HELP (4357). If stopping alcohol, benzodiazepines, or another substance could cause dangerous withdrawal, get medical help rather than trying to white-knuckle it alone.
