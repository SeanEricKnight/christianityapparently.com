I want to follow Jesus

Course 02 · 4 lessons

Read the Bible without pretending it is simple.

The Bible is a library, not a fortune cookie. Start with Jesus, learn to notice context, and do not panic when a difficult passage remains difficult.

About 35–50 minutesBring an actual Bible or BibleGateway
01

Lesson 1

Know what kind of book you are holding.

The Bible is a collection of history, poetry, prophecy, letters, wisdom writing, and the Gospels, written across centuries by real human authors. Christians also believe God speaks through it.

2 Timothy 3:16–17 · Luke 1:1–4 · 2 Peter 1:20–21 (NLT) ↗

Do this: read the Bible overview before trying to solve inspiration, manuscripts, contradictions, and canon all at once.

Read: Why trust the Bible? →Open BibleGateway ↗
02

Lesson 2

Start where Christianity starts: Jesus.

Begin with Mark. It moves quickly and keeps Jesus in front of you. Then read John, which spends more time on who Jesus claims to be.

Mark 1 · John 20:30–31 (NLT) ↗

Do this: read Mark 1 today. Ask: What does this passage actually say? What surprises me? What am I assuming that is not on the page?

Read Mark 1 ↗BibleProject overviews ↗
03

Lesson 3

Context is not cheating.

Words live inside paragraphs, books, genres, cultures, and historical moments. Reading a proverb like a promise or poetry like a police report will produce strange theology with impressive efficiency.

Luke 24:25–27 · 2 Timothy 2:15 (NLT) ↗

Do this: take one hard Bible question and read the surrounding chapter before reading anyone’s explanation of it.

Are there real contradictions? →Who decided the canon? →
04

Lesson 4

Let difficult passages stay difficult long enough to learn from them.

Some problems dissolve with context. Some do not. Mature reading is not pretending every objection has a three-sentence answer.

Acts 17:11 · 2 Peter 3:15–16 (NLT) ↗

Do this: read one morally difficult question, then compare the answer with the biblical passages it cites. Keep the text open while you evaluate the explanation.

Old Testament violence →What about slavery? →

Keep going

Make reading normal.

Read a little most days. Ask questions. Use good tools. Talk with other Christians. The goal is not to finish the Bible as if it were a software update.
