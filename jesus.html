Start with Him

Who is
Jesus?

Before Christianity was a religion you had an opinion about, there was Jesus.

If you’re going to decide what you think about Christianity, it seems fair to spend some time with the person at the center of it.

Start before the arguments.

Jesus of Nazareth was a first-century Jewish teacher who announced the kingdom of God, healed people, told uncomfortable stories, forgave sins, challenged religious hypocrisy, welcomed people respectable society preferred to avoid, and made claims about Himself that are difficult to reduce to “good moral teacher.”

Christians believe He is the Messiah, the Son of God, that He died for our sins, and that God raised Him from the dead.

You may not believe all of that. You don’t have to pretend you do in order to look.

Read what He said. Watch what He did. Pay attention to the people He moved toward.

Read one Gospel.

If you’ve never actually read one of the accounts of Jesus’ life, start there instead of trying to form an opinion from fragments, church memories, internet arguments, or Christians behaving badly.

Mark

Short, fast, and direct. A good place to meet Jesus without much preamble.

John

Slower and more reflective. John puts Jesus’ identity front and center and records long conversations you won’t find in the other Gospels.

Notice the strange parts.

Notice who feels comfortable around Him and who doesn’t. Notice how often He is compassionate toward obvious sinners and severe with people who are certain of their own righteousness.

Notice, too, that Jesus does not ask merely to be admired. He calls people to trust Him and follow Him.

If you want to begin

Jesus, I want to know who You are. Show me.

You do not have to solve Christianity before you can start looking at Christ.
