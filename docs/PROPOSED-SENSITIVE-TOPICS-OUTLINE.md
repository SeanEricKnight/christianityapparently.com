# Proposed outline: sexuality, marriage, singleness, women's roles

**Status: PROPOSAL ONLY. Nothing here is published.** No HTML pages exist for these topics, none are in `questions-data.json`, and none are in the sitemap. Please review the framing decisions below before any drafting.

Editorial brief: explain what Scripture says, where Christians disagree and why, and be honest about which readings have stronger historical and textual support. Do not pretend every interpretation is equally supported, and do not preach. Name the cost of each position on real people.

## Framing decisions I need from you

1. **The site's own position.** Existing pages already speak in the first person ("I think…") and use a "What I'm not sure about" section. Should these pages do the same, stating your view and then where Christians disagree? Or describe the main positions without a stated view?
2. **Tradition lens.** Which positions count as the historic majority view that the page presents first? (My suggestion: state plainly that the historic position across Catholic, Orthodox and most Protestant churches has been that sex belongs within marriage between a man and a woman, then present the revisionist and "side B" views fairly. You may want a different framing.)
3. **Voice on pastoral content.** Should each page end with a pastoral note for readers directly affected (gay and trans readers, divorced readers, single readers, women who have been told to be silent)?
4. **Review before publishing.** Do you want an outside reader (pastor, scholar, or LGBTQ Christian) to look at these before they go live?

## Sources to verify before drafting

- Primary: the Bible passages above in at least two translations; Didache 2; Augustine, *On the Good of Marriage*; Chrysostom on Junia (Homily 31 on Romans).
- Scholarship on both sides, chosen so each side is represented by its best books: for the traditional view, Robert Gagnon, *The Bible and Homosexual Practice* (2001), and Wesley Hill, *Washed and Waiting* (2010); for affirming readings, James Brownson, *Bible, Gender, Sexuality* (2013); for women's roles, Wayne Grudem and John Piper for complementarian, and Ronald Pierce and Rebecca Groothuis, or Craig Keener, for egalitarian. I haven't verified current editions, quotations, or the authors' present positions; this needs checking.
- Catholic: *Catechism of the Catholic Church* paragraphs on marriage and chastity (CCC 1601–1666, 2331–2400). Orthodox: a source to be chosen.
- Medical and social science claims (especially on gender dysphoria) should be cited only to authoritative reviews, with explicit uncertainty; I would not make empirical claims I can't source.

## Risks

- Anything here can hurt people on every side. Tone matters as much as content.
- The site's trust depends on not caricaturing the view the author disagrees with, whichever one it is.
- Search engines and social media will treat these as flashpoints; plan for hostile comments and a clear moderation approach.
- These pages should not be drafted by me alone. They should go through your own theological review and ideally an outside reader.

## What I would not do

- One page covering "LGBTQ issues." It will satisfy no one and mislead many.
- A false-balance page that presents all readings as equally supported by the text.
- Statistics, anecdotes or quotations I can't source.
