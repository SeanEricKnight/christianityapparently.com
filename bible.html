I want to follow Jesus

Scripture

Why should I
trust the Bible?

Start with what the Bible actually is.

The Bible isn’t one book that dropped out of the sky finished. It’s a collection of texts: history, poetry, prophecy, letters, wisdom writing, and the Gospels. Different authors wrote them over many centuries, in very different situations.

So what are Christians claiming?

Christians say Scripture is inspired by God. Traditions explain how that works a little differently, but historic Christianity doesn’t ask you to pretend the human authors weren’t there. Their vocabulary, style, setting, and personalities are all over the text.

Can we know what the text originally said?

The manuscripts were copied by hand, so they differ from each other in places. That’s not a secret. Textual criticism is the field where scholars compare manuscripts to work out the earliest wording they can recover.

Some of the differences are interesting. Most are minor. Having variants is a reason to look at the manuscript evidence. It isn’t, by itself, a reason to decide the text can’t be known.

What about the Gospels?

They’re ancient biographies of Jesus. Whether they’re reliable comes down to ordinary historical questions: when they were written, what sources they used, how close they were to the events, where they agree and where they differ, and whether they fit what we know from other evidence.

Those questions deserve real investigation. “The Bible says it, so that settles it” doesn’t do the work, and neither does “it’s an ancient text, so it’s myth.”

What about contradictions?

Some apparent contradictions go away once you consider context or genre. Others are real differences in detail, and those need an honest look.

A good answer doesn’t assume ahead of time that every difficulty is trivial. And one hard passage doesn’t prove the whole Bible is false.

And the morally disturbing parts?

The Bible has passages about violence, slavery, judgment, sex, war, and how outsiders are treated. A serious reader should be bothered enough to ask what’s going on.

They need historical, literary, and theological context. Sometimes the honest answer is that it’s still hard.

This page is a starting point. I’m not going to settle centuries of biblical scholarship on one web page. That would be suspiciously efficient.

Where should I start?

If what you really want to know about is Christianity, read one of the Gospels. Mark is short. John is slower and more reflective. Luke pays a lot of attention to outsiders and people who get overlooked.
